I'm in a stage where confusion comes to my mind.

I think there are some points one really needs to figure out in this special time.

I am writing this article to help me clarify one question:
"What is valuable for people to learn in the era of AI?"

The question Prof. Bas Spitters asked me in a short interview keeps echoing in my mind:
"What is the purpose of a PhD?"

I said, "I don't know."

But Bas told me that the purpose of starting a PhD is to have a period of time to work and think deeply.
This deeply inspired this article.

My conclusion to this question is:
"Before we step into the world where humans can still monitor AI, there are three things one needs to develop: mental models (dreaming), judgement, and taste."

To clarify, mental models (dreaming) refers to:

- asking good questions;
- catching nonsense.

Judgement and taste refer to:

- what's risky;
- when to stop.

Next, I'll spend the rest of the article explaining why they are the most important.

What do people want to learn?
People learn to survive.
This is an observation based on human history.

At the origin of human history, a story like this might have happened: an ape found that one can throw a stone onto solid ground to obtain many sharp pieces of stone.

With these pieces of stone, this ape could hunt larger animals or build a shelter.
He showed this technique to his family, who imitated his behavior.
The ape's family acquired this knowledge, and they could do more things.

In modern society, we have money as an intermediary for exchanging value.
We are living in a world of money and value.

According to the traditional economic view, value tends to move toward whatever is scarce.

So, to me, this question can be reduced to: what is most scarce now?

To analyse this, we need to clarify what we mean by learning here.

Here, by learning I mean a process after which one can do something that they couldn't do before.
And one important feature of learning here is that
people who notice this process can extract a repeatable form out of it.

Before the era of AI, it was hard to distinguish between remembering and understanding,
because they are a joint process.

Based on personal experience, understanding and remembering behave, to me, like electricity and magnetism.
Light travels through the air as orthogonal electric and magnetic fields generating each other.
People's learning also goes through this process.

However, in this era, AI minimises people's need to remember, and it has deeply enhanced the understanding process, since it can also generate ideas.

If we think digitally, in the digital world there's no difference between AI and humans.
Because not all the behavior that a human can perform can be fully simulated by AI in the physical world, pregnancy being an example.

Maybe years and years later we will have human-like robots that can become pregnant and produce new baby robots.
Then humans can worry about their existence (at least to me).

So the main worry actually comes from the fear of identity loss in the digital world, or of value loss in a society of value.

For AI, we see another world of injustice and inequality,
because different models behave differently, i.e., they vary in their level of remembering and understanding.
We are not going to talk about it here.

The most important thing here is dreaming.

By dreaming, I really mean asking questions about the future world you dream of.
Most people never ask questions about the future world they are dreaming of; instead, they arrange their future.

A question might arise here:
"Among the millions of possible worlds, which world do you want to see?"

So here we need to judge.

So your everyday routine can be changed into this: wake up and ask yourself, what do I want to be able to see and judge that I can't today?
